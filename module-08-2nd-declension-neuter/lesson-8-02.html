📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Why does one column of the table look different from the other two?
Yesterday you met the neuter rule for one noun, bellum. Today you set puella (1st decl. fem.), servus (2nd decl. masc.), and bellum (2nd decl. neut.) side by side — and see exactly where the rule shows up and where it never does.
📚 PART 1 · WHY THE NEUTER COLUMN COLLAPSES

In a feminine or masculine noun, the nominative and accusative are always two different endings — puella vs. puellam, servus vs. servum. Neuter nouns don't work that way. Long ago, in the ancestor language behind Latin, neuter nouns mostly named things rather than people, and things were rarely the agent of a sentence the way a person is. That old habit froze into a permanent rule: for every neuter noun, in every declension, the subject form and the object form are written exactly the same way.

You don't need to memorize that history. You do need to trust the rule — and know what it means for you as a reader: when you meet a neuter noun in a sentence, the ending alone won't tell you whether it's the subject or the object. You'll have to think about it. More on that soon.

🎵 MEMORY WORK · THE THREE-TYPE COMPARISON
Your target is this three-column table — every noun type you've studied so far, nominative and accusative, singular and plural. Chant it aloud, one column at a time:
1st Decl. Fem. (puella)2nd Decl. Masc. (servus)2nd Decl. Neut. (bellum)
Nom. sg.puellaservusbellum *
Acc. sg.puellamservumbellum *
Nom. pl.puellaeservībella *
Acc. pl.puellāsservōsbella *
The four cells marked * are the neuter cells — nominative and accusative identical in both singular and plural. No other column has a single starred cell.
💡 Key observation to memorize: The neuter nominative and accusative forms are identical in both singular and plural. No other declension shares this feature.

📚 PART 3 · FIXED BY THE ENDING, OR NOT?

Dominus templum amat. (The master loves the temple.) A person — dominus — is the one doing the loving, so templum must be the thing being loved, even though its ending alone can't prove that.
