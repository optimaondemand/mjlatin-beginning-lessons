📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
How do all three noun types fit together into one system?
Welcome to Module 15 — the last stop before the Semester Exam. No new grammar begins today. Instead, this week you'll pull everything you've built since Module 1 into one place: three noun paradigms, a full verb system, and every skill you've practiced along the way. Today's job is to lay all three noun paradigms side by side and see, at last, the whole shape of the system.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to lay out the nom./acc. sg./pl. paradigm for all three noun types you've studied: 1st declension, 2nd declension masculine, and 2nd declension neuter.
2
I will be able to tell, from an ending alone, which of the three noun types a word belongs to.
3
I will be able to recognize the semester's highlight vocabulary and the English words it left behind.

💡 The neuter rule: in the third table, notice that nominative and accusative are always identical — singular and plural. This is true of every neuter noun in Latin. Context, not the ending, tells you whether a neuter noun is subject or object.
One irregularity worth remembering: agricola (farmer) looks like a 1st-declension noun — it ends in -a and takes 1st-declension endings — but it names a man, so it is grammatically masculine. Ending and gender don't always match; the dictionary entry tells you which is which.
