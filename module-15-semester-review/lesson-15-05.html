Are you ready for the Semester Exam?

📚 PART 1 · A ROMAN FARM — READ IT THE ROMAN WAY

Run the full parsing checklist from Lesson 15.04 on this passage. Every form here is built entirely from what you already know — there is nothing new, only more of it at once.

Agricola in agrō habitat. Fīlius agricolae servōs vocat. Servī et fīlius ad oppidum properant. Per viās dona ad templum portant. Templum magnum est. Populus Rōmānus in forō spectat. Agricola et fīlius dona deīs dant. (agrō = in the field — ablative, given; deīs = to the gods — dative, given)
Read it out: “The farmer lives in the field. The farmer's son calls the slaves. The slaves and the son hurry toward the town. Through the roads they carry gifts to the temple. The temple is great. The Roman people watch in the forum. The farmer and the son give gifts to the gods.”
Notice how much of this you parsed without help: agricola (nom. sg., subject), servōs (acc. pl., object of vocat), servī et fīlius (compound nom. pl. subject), properant (3rd pl., agreeing), ad oppidum and per viās (accusative after prepositions), dona (nom./acc. pl. neuter — the neuter rule again), templum and magnum (nom. sg. neuter, predicate), populus Rōmānus (nom. sg. masc.). The ablative and dative forms are glossed — you'll study those cases in Semester 2.
🎮 PLAY THE LUDUS
Ludus XV · Certāmen Fīnāle I

This is the biggest game of the semester: twenty items pulling from every noun type, all fourteen verbs, esse, and the accusative prepositions, all mixed together in one 120-second round. Play it as many times as you like — your best score counts, and it's the fastest way to make the whole system automatic before the exam.

📚 PART 3 · WHAT THE SEMESTER EXAM WILL ASK OF YOU

The Semester Exam has no surprises in it — it draws entirely from what you've reviewed this week, organized into four familiar kinds of questions. Try each sample below; if it feels manageable, you are ready.

✅ SAMPLE EXAM FORMATS
Each question below is the same type you'll see on the exam. Give it a try.
Paradigm completion: the accusative plural of bellum, bellī (n.) is …
Parsing: in Servī dominum vocant, what is servī?
Translation: Fīlius agricolae oppidum spectat.
Derivatives: which English word comes from Latin terra?
💡 That's the whole exam. Paradigm completion, parsing, translation, and derivatives — four question types you've now practiced dozens of times this week alone, and hundreds of times across the semester. You are more ready than you think.
