📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Can you translate sentences that mix everything you've learned?
Every sentence below could have come from any module this semester — girls and gates from Module 5, slaves and masters from Module 7–8, farmers and verbs from Module 10–12. Translating them all together is the real test of whether the system has come together in your head.
📚 PART 1 · MIXED TRANSLATION PRACTICE

Choose the best English translation for each sentence.

✅ CHECK YOUR UNDERSTANDING
Translate each sentence.
Servī dominum laudant.
Oppida Romānorum magna sunt.
Puellae et fēminae aquam portant.
Agricolae et servī ad oppidum properant.
Rōma urbs magna est. (urbs = city — given)
📚 PART 2 · ROMAN WORLD HIGHLIGHTS

A quick tour back through the Roman world this semester introduced you to: the forum, the beating heart of public, legal, and religious life; the aqueducts that carried water for miles on stone arches; the military copiae that Caesar led into Gaul; and the values of gloria, victoria, and patria that motivated Roman public life. Which one stuck with you the most?

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
Which piece of Roman culture from this semester do you find most memorable?

💪 ONE IDEA TO CARRY OUT

Translating well isn't about knowing more vocabulary — it's about reading the endings first. Subject, then object, then verb agreement, in that order, every time.
