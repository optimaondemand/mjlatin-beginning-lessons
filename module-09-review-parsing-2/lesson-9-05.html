📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Are you ready for the Module 9 gate-check quiz?
No new grammar today — just pulling the whole week together: the three-column paradigm table, the neuter rule, and a mixed-declension passage read the Roman way. Then play the Ludus and take a self-check before the quiz.
📚 PART 1 · THE WEEK ON ONE PAGE
The paradigm — one system, three columns:
1st Decl. Fem.2nd Decl. Masc.2nd Decl. Neut.
Nom. Sg.-a-us-um
Acc. Sg.-am-um-um
Nom. Pl.-ae-ī-a
Acc. Pl.-ās-ōs-a
Neuter rule: nominative and accusative are always identical for a 2nd decl. neuter noun.
Agreement: one subject → est · many → sunt.
💡 When in doubt, check the dictionary entry. Genitive singular -ae means 1st declension; genitive singular -ī means 2nd declension. The entry's gender label (f., m., n.) resolves every overlap.
📚 PART 2 · READ IT THE ROMAN WAY

Run the full parsing checklist on this short passage. It uses all three declensions — find every noun's declension, case, number, and function:

Gallia magna terra est. Populus Romanus Galliam amat. Oppida et templa multa sunt. Senatus bellum non amat. (magna, Romanus, multa = adjectives “large,” “Roman,” “many,” given)
Read it out: “Gaul is a great land. The Roman people love Gaul. There are many fortified towns and temples. The Senate does not love war.”
Notice: Gallia and terra are both nom. sg., 1st fem. (subject and predicate nominative). Galliam is acc. sg., 1st fem. (direct object). Oppida et templa is a plural neuter subject (-a, neuter rule) — so the verb is sunt. Bellum is acc. sg., 2nd neut. — by the neuter rule alone you can't tell subject from object, but the sentence tells you it's what the Senate does not love.

🎮 PLAY THE LUDUS
Ludus IX · Recognitiō II

This week's game is bigger: 20 mixed forms from all three declensions, 120 seconds on the clock. For each form, pick the parse that's actually correct — the wrong answer is always a close, plausible mix-up, so read carefully. Play as many times as you like — your best score counts. It's the fastest way to make mixed-declension parsing automatic before the quiz.
