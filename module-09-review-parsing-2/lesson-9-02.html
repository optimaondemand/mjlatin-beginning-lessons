📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Can you say all three paradigms without stopping to think?
Today's whole job is the Memory Work: all three paradigm tables, side by side, chanted until they come without hesitation. This is the single most important lesson of the week — everything later in this module depends on this table being automatic.
🎵 MEMORY WORK · THE THREE-COLUMN TABLE
Your target: recite every cell of this table from memory, in this order, for all three columns.
1st Decl. Fem.2nd Decl. Masc.2nd Decl. Neut.
Nom. Sg.-a-us-um
Acc. Sg.-am-um-um
Nom. Pl.-ae-ī-a
Acc. Pl.-ās-ōs-a
Example words: puella / puellam / puellae / puellās · servus / servum / servī / servōs · bellum / bellum / bella / bella.
Column chant: cover the right two columns and recite the 1st declension column top to bottom (-a, -am, -ae, -ās). Uncover column two; recite 2nd masc. (-us, -um, -ī, -ōs). Uncover column three; recite 2nd neut. (-um, -um, -a, -a). Then cover all three and recite from memory.

📚 PART 2 · THE THREE CRITICAL OVERLAPS

Three cells in your table produce forms that look identical across declensions. Knowing them by name is what keeps you from guessing wrong.

Overlap 1: 2nd decl. masc. accusative singular = 2nd decl. neut. nom./acc. singular. Both are -um. Servum (2nd masc., acc. sg.) and bellum (2nd neut., nom./acc. sg.) look identical — only the dictionary entry's gender label tells them apart.
Overlap 2: 2nd decl. neut. plural is always -a (bella, templa) — never -ae. 1st decl. nominative plural is always -ae (puellae) — never -a. Read the ending carefully; it's easy to blur the two when reading quickly.
Overlap 3: a form ending in -a could be 1st decl. nominative singular (puella) or 2nd decl. neuter plural (bella). The dictionary entry resolves it every time: puella, puellae f. versus bellum, bellī n.
