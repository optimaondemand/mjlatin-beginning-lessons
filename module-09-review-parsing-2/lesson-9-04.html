Lesson 9.04
The Full Parsing Checklist

📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Can you give a complete parse for any form — every time?
This is the hardest practice of the week: mixed forms from all three declensions, with no clues about which is which. You'll run the full checklist — declension, case, number, and function — on each one, and you'll practice catching a parsing error before it becomes a habit.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to parse any 1st or 2nd declension nom./acc. form completely: declension, case, and number.
2
I will be able to identify a noun's grammatical function — subject, direct object, or predicate nominative — from context.
3
I will be able to recognize a form that belongs to a case I haven't studied yet, and say so honestly instead of guessing.
📚 PART 1 · THE FULL PARSING CHECKLIST

Run these steps on every noun you meet, in order:

1
Read the ending. If you're not sure which declension it belongs to, check the dictionary entry.
2
Name the declension (1st fem., 2nd masc., or 2nd neut.).
3
Name the case and number (nom./acc., sg./pl.).
4
If it's neuter, remember the neuter rule: nominative and accusative are the same form. Say “nom./acc.” rather than guessing one or the other.
5
Use the sentence to decide the noun's function: subject, direct object, or predicate nominative (nominative after est/sunt).
📚 PART 2 · HARDEST MIXED PRACTICE

No hints this time beyond the dictionary entry. Give the complete parse.

✅ CHECK YOUR UNDERSTANDING
Choose the complete, correct parse for each form.
terram (terra, terrae f.)
oppida (oppidum, oppidī n.)
servī (servus, servī m.)
aquās (aqua, aquae f.)
dominorum (dominus, dominī m.)
📚 PART 3 · FUNCTION AND ERROR-CATCHING

Parsing a form correctly is only half the job — you also need its function in the sentence. Look at this worked example:

Puella templum amat. — The girl loves the temple.

Puella is nominative singular, 1st fem. — the subject. Templum is accusative singular, 2nd neut. — the direct object. Same neuter rule applies: if you saw templum alone, you couldn't tell nominative from accusative — but here the sentence tells you it's receiving the action of amat.

Now here's a common student error: a student parses bella (from bellum, bellī n.) as “accusative singular, 1st declension feminine.” The error: bella ends in -a on a neuter noun, which signals nominative or accusative plural — not singular, and not 1st declension, since the dictionary entry is neuter.

✅ CHECK YOUR UNDERSTANDING
Identify the noun's function or catch the parsing error.
Dominus viam amat. — What is viam?
Bellum regnum est. — What is regnum?
A student parses silvae (from silva, silvae f.) as “accusative plural.” What is the error?
