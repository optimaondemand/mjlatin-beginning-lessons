📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
If amō is “I love,” how do you say “we love”?
In Module 10 you learned the three singular endings of the present tense: -ō, -ās, -at. But a verb needs to be able to talk about more than one person doing the action, too. Today you meet the three plural endings that finish the pattern — and complete the whole present tense paradigm.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name the three plural personal endings of the 1st conjugation present tense: -āmus, -ātis, -ant.
2
I will be able to explain that these endings work exactly like the singular ones: they replace a subject pronoun.
3
I will be able to tell a singular verb form from a plural one on sight.

📚 PART 2 · THREE MORE ENDINGS

The stem doesn't change — only the ending

Remember the present stem of amō, amāre is amā-. In Module 10 you attached -ō, -ās, -at to it. Now attach three new endings to the very same stem:

amāmus
we love  👩‍🦶‍🦺
amātis
you all love
amant
they love

Just like the singular endings, each plural ending replaces a subject pronoun. You do not need a separate word for “we,” “you all,” or “they” — the ending already says it.

💡 Heads-up: this same swap works for every 1st-conjugation verb you know from Module 10 — laudō, portō, habitō, navigō, superō, laborō — not just amō. Learn the pattern once; it works for all of them.
