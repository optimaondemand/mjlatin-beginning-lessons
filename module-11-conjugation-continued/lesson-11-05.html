Are you ready for the Module 11 Quiz?
No new grammar today — just pulling the week together: the full six-form paradigm, seven new verbs, and compound-subject agreement. Read a short passage the Roman way, play the Ludus, and take a self-check.
📚 PART 1 · THE WEEK ON ONE PAGE
The complete present tense paradigm — every 1st-conjugation verb you know:
PersonSingularPlural
1st-ō-āmus
2nd-ās-ātis
3rd-at-ant
14 verbs, one pattern: amō, laudō, portō, habitō, navigō, superō, laborō, spectō, errō, properō, parō, putō, vocō — plus the slightly irregular dō.
Compound subjects: two nominatives joined by et = plural subject = -nt verb.
📚 PART 2 · READ IT THE ROMAN WAY

Run the reader's checklist on this short passage. Find each subject, check its true number, and confirm the verb agrees:

Nautae et agricolae spectant. Fēmina et puella dōna parant. Servus ad forum properat, sed puer errat. Magister puellās vocat.
Read it out: “The sailors and farmers watch. The woman and girl prepare gifts. The slave hurries to the forum, but the boy wanders. The teacher calls the girls.”
Nautae et agricolae and fēmina et puella are compound subjects — both plural, both take -nt. Servus and puer are each singular and joined only by sed (not et), so each keeps its own singular verb. Magister is singular; puellās is the accusative plural object.
