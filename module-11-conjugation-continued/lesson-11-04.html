Two Become One Subject

📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
What happens to the verb when two subjects share one action?
Two singular nouns joined by et (and) act as a single compound subject — and Latin treats that compound subject as plural, exactly the way esse already taught you. You'll also meet dō, the one verb in this module that breaks the pattern slightly.
🎵 MEMORY WORK
The irregular paradigm of dō, dāre — notice the short a (no macron) in every form except dās:
PersonSingularPlural
1stdō — I givedamus — we give
2nddās — you givedatis — you all give
3rddat — he/she/it givesdant — they give
Compare: amāmus, amātis (long ā) vs. damus, datis (short a). This is the most common mistake beginners make with dō — watch for it.

📚 PART 1 · ONE SUBJECT, TWO NOUNS
Nauta laborat.
The sailor works. — one subject
Nauta et agricola laborant.
The sailor and the farmer work. — compound subject

Nauta and agricola are each nominative singular nouns on their own. But joined by et, together they act as more than one — so the verb takes the 3rd person plural ending, -nt.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
In English, would you say “Tom and Jerry is here” or “Tom and Jerry are here”? What does your ear tell you?
📚 PART 2 · THE ONE VERB THAT BREAKS THE PATTERN

Dō is still a 1st-conjugation verb — it uses the same six endings — but its stem vowel is short almost everywhere. The only long form is dās. Because dō is one of the oldest, most frequently used words in the language, it kept an irregularity that a newer verb would have smoothed away.

💡 Bonus pattern: you already know sumus, estis, sunt from esse (Module 8). Compare them to amāmus, amātis, amant: the endings -mus, -tis, -nt show up in both. If you ever forget a 1st-conjugation plural ending, you can reason from esse as your anchor.
📚 PART 3 · THE READER'S CHECKLIST

When you read a Latin sentence, run this quick check:

1
Find the subject (the nominative noun or nouns). Singular, or joined by et?
2
Check the verb ending. Does it match the subject's true number?
3
If the verb is dō, remember: short a everywhere except dās.
