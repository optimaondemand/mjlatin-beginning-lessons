📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
What does a dictionary entry actually tell you?
Every Latin noun's dictionary entry packs three facts into a tiny space. Today you learn to read it — and meet a word that seems to break the rules.
🎵 MEMORY WORK
Your target is the paradigm cell you're building — just one cell so far. Chant it aloud:
Singular
Nominative (subject)-a
Say: “First declension, nominative singular: -a.”
Now recite the dictionary entry pattern:
nominative singular, genitive singular + gender abbreviation
puella, puellae f.
nauta, nautae m.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to read a 1st-declension dictionary entry and name its three parts.
2
I will be able to explain what the genitive singular ending -ae signals in a dictionary entry.
3
I will be able to recognize that a noun's gender must come from the dictionary entry, not from its ending.
📚 PART 1 · THREE FACTS, ONE ENTRY

Open a Latin dictionary to puella, puellae f. and you're looking at three separate facts, packed into one line:

FACT 1
puella
nominative singular — the subject form you'll use most
FACT 2
puellae
genitive singular — the declension signal
FACT 3
f.
gender abbreviation

You won't translate the genitive until Semester 2. But you need to recognize it right now, because it's the key that unlocks a noun's declension. If a noun's genitive ends in -ae, it follows the same pattern as puella.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
Look at the entry silva, silvae f. Before any more instruction — what do you think silvae is doing there?
📚 PART 2 · NOT EVERY -A NOUN IS FEMININE

Meet the exception: nauta

Most 1st-declension nouns are feminine. But nauta (sailor) is masculine — and so are a few other 1st-declension nouns you'll meet later, mostly names for jobs Roman culture associated with men. The dictionary entry is the only reliable way to know a noun's gender.

puella, puellae f.
girl — feminine, as expected
nauta, nautae m.
sailor — masculine, despite the -a ending
💡 Rule of thumb: the ending tells you the job of a noun in a sentence. The dictionary entry — not the ending — tells you the gender.

💪 ONE IDEA TO CARRY OUT

A dictionary entry has three parts: nominative singular, genitive singular (the declension signal), and gender. You can't guess gender from -a alone — always check the entry.
