📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
How does a noun's ending signal its job in a sentence?
In English, word order tells you who is doing what: “The girl sees the woman” means something different from “The woman sees the girl.” Latin nouns work differently — they carry their job right in their ending. Today you meet the ending that says “I am the subject”: -a.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to explain what inflection means and how it is different from English word order.
2
I will be able to recognize the 1st-declension nominative singular ending, -a.
3
I will be able to identify the subject of a simple Latin sentence with esse.

📚 PART 2 · THE ENDING THAT MEANS “SUBJECT”

The nominative singular ending is -a

Because Latin nouns are inflected, a Roman writer could rearrange the words in a sentence without changing who did what. The ending on the noun does that work instead of word order. The ending -a on a 1st-declension noun tells you: this noun is the subject — or, with a form of esse, it describes the subject.

puella
girl — nominative singular  👶
terra
land, earth — nominative singular  🌎
💡 Heads-up for later: you'll meet another ending, -am, that looks similar but marks a completely different job — the object. That's Module 4. For now, -a means subject (or a description of the subject).
