Lesson 3.04
Subject or Predicate?
Lesson
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Can one nominative noun have two different jobs?
You already know esse in all six present-tense forms. Today you combine that with the nominative case to build complete sentences — and learn its second job: the predicate nominative.
🎵 MEMORY WORK
The nominative's two jobs: subject — the noun the sentence is about  ·  predicate nominative — the noun after esse that renames or describes the subject. Both jobs use the same -a ending.
Don't forget: nauta is masculine, even though it ends in -a.
📚 PART 1 · ONE ENDING, TWO JOBS
Puella est.
The girl is (here). — puella is the subject
Puella femina est.
The girl is a woman. — puella = subject; femina = predicate nominative

When a noun follows a form of esse and renames or describes the subject, it's called a predicate nominative. It's still the nominative case — still the same -a ending — just a different job.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
In English we say “She is a sailor” — the word “sailor” renames “she.” Which English word here works the way a Latin predicate nominative works?
📚 PART 2 · TELLING THEM APART

When you read a sentence with two nominative nouns, run this quick check:

1
Find the noun(s) ending in -a.
2
Find esse. Ask: which noun is the sentence really about? That one is the subject.
3
The other nominative noun — the one that renames or describes the subject — is the predicate nominative.
💡 Watch out: both words carry the exact same -a ending, so you can't tell subject from predicate nominative by the ending alone — you have to read the whole sentence.
📚 PART 3 · SUBJECT OR PREDICATE NOMINATIVE?

For the underlined word in each sentence, choose its job.
