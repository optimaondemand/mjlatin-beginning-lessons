📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Can you parse a whole paragraph, one sentence at a time?
Every sentence you've read so far has stood alone. Today you meet your first paragraph — six connected Latin sentences. The good news: Latin's endings keep reminding you of every grammatical relationship, sentence by sentence, so reading a paragraph is not harder than reading one sentence six times. It just asks you to keep going.
📚 PART 1 · THE FULL PARSING CHECKLIST

Run this four-step check on every sentence you read from now on:

1
Find the subject. Parse it: case, number, declension.
2
Find the verb. Parse it: person, number, tense, conjugation (or note “esse”).
3
Find the direct object, if there is one. Parse it the same way as the subject.
4
Confirm agreement: does the verb's person and number match the subject's number?
💡 Once you can run this checklist automatically, translating stops being guesswork and becomes something closer to solving — every clue is right there in the endings.
📚 PART 2 · THE PARAGRAPH

Read it through once for the general idea — every word here is one you already know:

Rōmānī magnī sunt. Rōmānī Italiam habitant. Agricolae terram amant et laborant. Nautae trāns aquam navigant et terram laudant. Servī dōna portant. Puellae et servī aquam amant.
Read it out: “The Romans are great. The Romans inhabit Italy. The farmers love the land and work (it). The sailors sail across the water and praise the land. The slaves carry the gifts. The girls and the slaves love the water.”

Two sentences, worked out completely:

Sentence 1Parsing
Rōmānīnom. pl., 2nd decl., subject
magnīnom. pl., 2nd decl. adjective, predicate nominative
sunt3rd pl., present, esse — links subject to predicate nominative
Sentence 3Parsing
Agricolaenom. pl., 1st decl., subject
terramacc. sg., 1st decl., direct object (shared by both verbs)
amant…laborantboth 3rd pl., present, 1st conj. — one subject, two verbs joined by et
📚 PART 3 · PARSE THE REST OF THE PARAGRAPH

Now try the remaining sentences yourself.

✅ CHECK YOUR UNDERSTANDING
Answer each one, then read the feedback.
In Rōmānī magnī sunt, what is sunt?
In Agricolae terram amant et laborant, what does et join?
In Nautae trāns aquam navigant et terram laudant, what is terram?
In Puellae et servī aquam amant, why is amant plural?

💪 ONE IDEA TO CARRY OUT

A paragraph is just several sentences, and each one still carries its own grammar in its endings. Run the four-step checklist on each sentence, and the paragraph takes care of itself.
