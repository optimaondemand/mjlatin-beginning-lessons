📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Are you ready for the Module 12 Quiz?
No new grammar today — just pulling the week together: the full verb paradigm, the agreement rule, and the parsing checklist. Read one more short passage the Roman way, play the Ludus, and take a self-check.
📚 PART 1 · THE WEEK ON ONE PAGE
The full paradigm — one pattern, every 1st-conjugation verb:
PersonSingularPlural
1stamōamāmus
2ndamāsamātis
3rdamatamant
Two formulas: nouns — case, number, declension, function · verbs — person, number, tense, conjugation.
Agreement: a plural subject always needs a plural verb; a singular subject always needs a singular verb — even in a compound subject joined by et.
📚 PART 2 · READ IT THE ROMAN WAY

Run the parsing checklist on this short passage. Find each subject, check its number, and confirm the verb agrees:

Rōmānī magnī sunt. Caesar Galliam superat. Servī dōna portant.
Read it out: “The Romans are great. Caesar overcomes Gaul. The slaves carry the gifts.”
Three sentences, three different structures: predicate nominative (sunt), transitive with a proper-noun subject (superat), and a plain subject-object-verb sentence (portant) — and every verb agrees with its subject.
