📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
What will the Semester Exam actually ask you to do?
No new grammar this week — just a calm, clear look at what's ahead. Every single thing on the Semester Exam is something you have already done in a lesson or a quiz this semester. Today you'll walk through exactly what the exam covers, so that when you sit down to take it, nothing on the page surprises you.
🎵 MEMORY WORK · SAY THE ROADMAP
Before you look at anything else, say this out loud — the six-stop journey of your whole semester:
Sounds and stress → the inflection principle → 1st declension → 2nd declension → esse → 1st conjugation.
📚 PART 1 · SIX PARTS, ONE EXAM

The Semester Exam has six sections. Each one asks you to do something you have practiced dozens of times already — just now, all in one sitting.

1
Paradigm Identification — “Which ending is this?” You'll spot the right nominative or accusative ending for 1st- and 2nd-declension nouns.
2
Case Parsing — “What job is this word doing?” You'll name the case, number, and function (subject, direct object, or predicate nominative) of a word in a sentence.
3
Verb Forms — “Whose action is this?” You'll recognize person and number in esse and 1st-conjugation present-tense verbs.
4
Translation, Latin → English — You'll turn a Latin sentence into natural English.
5
Translation, English → Latin — You'll turn an English sentence into correctly inflected Latin.
6
Grammar Concepts — You'll explain why Latin works the way it does — the inflection principle, the neuter rule, and how the pieces fit together.
✅ SPOT THE SECTION
For each short description, name the exam section it belongs to.
“Tell me the case, number, and function of puellam in this sentence.”
“Write the Latin for: The slaves work.”
“Why does a neuter noun look the same in the nominative and accusative?”
📚 PART 2 · HOW THE EXAM WORKS

A few plain facts about the exam itself, so there's nothing to wonder about:

Auto-graded. You'll see your score as soon as you submit — no waiting.
40 points, 80% to advance. A score of 32/40 or higher shows you're ready for Semester 2.
It matters, but it's not a trap. It's worth 15% of your overall course grade — its own category, separate from your regular Module Quizzes.
💡 Full details on taking the exam — including what happens if you don't reach 80% on the first try — are waiting for you in Lesson 16.05, right before you begin.

📚 PART 3 · A LOOK BACK, BEFORE YOU LOOK AHEAD

Fifteen modules ago, Latin was a set of unfamiliar sounds. Look at what you've built since then:

You can pronounce Latin correctly on sight.
You know that endings, not word order, carry meaning.
You can inflect two full noun declensions.
You can conjugate a full Latin verb in the present tense.
You've read real, adapted Latin about the Roman world.

That's not a small list. This week is about proving it — to the exam, and to yourself.
