📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
How do you know which syllable to stress in a Latin word?
Every Latin word has a predictable beat. Once you can split a word into syllables and find its penult, you can stress any Latin word correctly — even one you've never seen before.
🎵 MEMORY WORK
Counting from the end of a word: the last syllable is the ultima, the second-to-last is the penult, the third-to-last is the antepenult.
The rule: if the penult is long, stress it. If the penult is short, stress the antepenult instead.
📚 PART 1 · SPLITTING WORDS INTO SYLLABLES

Two simple rules divide any Latin word:

1
A single consonant between two vowels goes with the following vowel: Ro–ma, I–ta–li–a.
2
Two or more consonants between vowels split — the first stays with the syllable before it: Gal–li–a, Bri–tan–ni–a.
💡 A diphthong (ae, au, oe) always counts as one vowel when you're splitting syllables — it never gets split apart.
📚 PART 2 · THE PENULT RULE IN ACTION

A syllable is “long” if it has a long vowel, a diphthong, or ends in two consonants. Watch the rule work on two words:

I–ta–li–a
Penult -li- is short → stress moves back to the antepenult: IT–a–li–a
Bri–tan–ni–a
Penult -tan- ends in two consonants (long by position) → stress stays on the penult: bri–TAN–ni–a

One more wrinkle worth knowing: a two-syllable word is always stressed on its first syllable — the penult rule doesn't even need to be checked.

📚 PART 3 · PRONOUNCE IT WITH CONFIDENCE

Run this quick check on any new Latin word:

1
Split the word into syllables. Find the penult (second-to-last).
2
Is the penult long (long vowel, diphthong, or two consonants after it)?
3
Long penult → stress it. Short penult → stress the antepenult instead.
