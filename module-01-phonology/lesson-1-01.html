📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
How does a language sound before you understand it?
Welcome to Latin! Before Julius Caesar's words were ever written down, they were spoken — in the Senate, in the Forum, on the march. To read Latin well, you first learn to hear it. Today you start with the building block of every sound: the vowel.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to explain why Latin was first a spoken language, not just a written one.
2
I will be able to tell a short Latin vowel from a long one by looking for the macron.
3
I will be able to explain why a macron can change a word's whole meaning.

📚 PART 2 · A LANGUAGE OF SOUND, NOT JUST SPELLING

Every Latin vowel is either short or long

English barely marks this difference. Latin marks it on every single vowel. A long vowel is held about twice as long as a short one — and it gets a tiny flat mark called a macron: ā, ē, ī, ō, ū.

ShortSounds like…LongSounds like…

💡 Not decoration: malum (short a) means evil. Mālum (long ā) means apple. To a Roman ear these are two totally different words — the macron carries real meaning, not just a pronunciation tip.
