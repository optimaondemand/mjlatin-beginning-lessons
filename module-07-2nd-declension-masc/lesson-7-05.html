📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Are you ready for the Module 7 Quiz?
No new grammar today — just pulling the week together: the full 2nd-declension masculine paradigm, the two exceptions, and mixed-declension sentences. Read a short passage the Roman way, play the Ludus, and take a self-check.
📚 PART 1 · THE WEEK ON ONE PAGE
The paradigm — 2nd declension masculine, every regular noun:
SingularPlural
Nominative — subject-us-ī
Accusative — object-um-ōs
Exception 1: vir — no -us in the nominative singular.
Exception 2: agricola, nauta — 1st-declension endings, masculine gender.
📚 PART 2 · READ IT THE ROMAN WAY

Run the mixed-declension checklist on this short passage. Find each noun, check its declension and ending, then confirm the meaning:

Agricola et nauta amicī sunt. Dominus servōs et filiōs amat. Virī Rōmānī sunt.
Read it out: “The farmer and the sailor are friends. The master loves the slaves and the sons. The men are Romans.”
Agricola and nauta look 1st declension but are masculine (joined by et, together a plural subject with sunt); servōs and filiōs show the accusative plural of the 2nd declension as objects of the singular dominus ... amat; virī shows the regular nominative plural of the noun that takes no -us in the singular.
