📚 PART 1 · READING A DICTIONARY ENTRY

Every Latin noun has a dictionary entry built the same way. Here is servus:

servus, servī m. — slave, servant
1
The first form (servus) is the nominative singular — the subject form, the one you look up.
2
The second form (servī) is the genitive singular. You will not need the genitive case itself until a later module — for now it just confirms the noun's spelling and stem.
3
The m. tells you the grammatical gender — masculine. You memorize this along with the word every time.
📚 PART 2 · THREE MORE MEN OF THE HOUSEHOLD

The same -us / -um pattern works for every 2nd-declension masculine noun. Add these to your notebook:

dominus, dominī m. — master, lord
filius, filiī m. — son
amicus, amicī m. — friend
Filius est.
The son is (here). — subject
Dominus amicum amat.
The master loves the friend. — amicum is the object
