📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Are you ready for the Module 4 Quiz?
No new grammar today — just pulling the week together: the growing paradigm table, the direct object, the five accusative-only prepositions. Read a short passage the Roman way, play the Ludus, and take a self-check.
📚 PART 1 · THE WEEK ON ONE PAGE
The paradigm — every 1st-declension noun, singular:
EndingFunction
Nominative-aSubject; Predicate Nominative
Accusative-amDirect Object; Object of Accusative Preposition
The verb: amat (loves, likes) always looks for a direct object.
The five: ad, per, trans, ante, post — always take the accusative.
📚 PART 2 · READ IT THE ROMAN WAY

Run the reader's checklist on this short passage. Find each noun, check its case, and confirm its job:

Nauta patriam amat. Puella viam amat. Via ad insulam est. Silva post viam est. Femina trans aquam est.
Read it out: “The sailor loves his homeland. The girl loves the road. The road is toward the island. The forest is behind the road. The woman is across the water.”
patriam and viam are direct objects of amat. insulam follows ad; viam follows post; aquam follows trans — all accusative. Nauta, puella, via, silva, and femina stay nominative because they are the subjects.
