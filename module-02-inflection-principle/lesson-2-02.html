📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
What is the one Latin verb you cannot get through a single sentence without?
Today's verb is esse — “to be.” It is the single most common verb in Latin, and you'll meet it constantly. Unlike most Latin verbs, it doesn't follow the tidy patterns you'll learn later — it simply has to be memorized, cold, right now.
🎵 MEMORY WORK
Your target is the complete present tense of esse — all six forms. Chant it aloud, singular row first, then plural:

1stsumI amsumuswe are
2ndesyou areestisyou (all) are
3rdesthe/she/it issuntthey are
Chant the six forms in order until they're automatic: sum — es — est — sumus — estis — sunt. Then say it backwards.

📚 PART 1 · THE MOST IRREGULAR, MOST IMPORTANT VERB

Every Latin student, in every century since Latin was first taught, has had to memorize these same six forms. Esse is the most frequently used verb in Latin — and also the most irregular. Most Latin verbs you'll meet later follow predictable patterns. Esse does not.

This is nothing new to you: “to be” is irregular in English too. “I am,” “you are,” “he is” — three different-looking words for one verb, because “to be” is so old and so common that it never smoothed itself into a tidy pattern. Latin's sum, es, est… is the same story. There's no trick here — just memorize it now, completely, and it will serve you for the rest of your studies.

📚 PART 2 · PERSON AND NUMBER

The six forms aren't random — they're organized by two questions: person (who's speaking — 1st = the speaker, 2nd = the listener, 3rd = someone/something else) and number (singular = one, plural = more than one).

Singular (one)Plural (more than one)
1st personsum (I am)sumus (we are)
2nd persones (you are)estis (you all are)
3rd personest (he/she/it is)sunt (they are)
💡 This six-cell grid — 1st/2nd/3rd person × singular/plural — organizes every Latin verb you'll study this year. Master the grid now with esse, and it will already feel familiar when regular verbs arrive.
