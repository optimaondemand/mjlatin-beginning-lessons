📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Are you ready to read real Latin sentences about the Roman world?
No new rules today — just pulling the whole week together: inflection, the esse paradigm, and the predicate nominative. Read a short passage of genuine Latin sentences, play the Ludus, and take a self-check before the Module 2 Quiz.
📚 PART 1 · THE WEEK ON ONE PAGE
Inflection: Latin marks a noun's job by changing its ending, not by word order — the same instinct English keeps alive in pronouns like he/him.
Esse: sum, es, est, sumus, estis, sunt — the most irregular, most essential verb in the language.
Predicate nominative: when esse links two nouns, both take the nominative case — subject and predicate are the same thing.
Vocabulary: Italia, Graecia, Gallia, Britannia, Hispania, Sicilia, Africa, Roma, insula, provincia.
📚 PART 2 · A SHORT ROMAN READING

Read each sentence below the way a Roman geographer might have written it — find the subject, find the predicate nominative, and confirm est or sunt matches the subject before you translate.

Roma provincia est. · Italia provincia est. · Gallia provincia est. · Sicilia insula est. · Britannia provincia est. · Hispania provincia est.
Reader's checklist: for each sentence, can you (1) name the subject, (2) name the predicate nominative, and (3) give an English translation? Try all five before checking your work with a partner or your teacher.

One more sentence worth knowing, straight from your Memory Work: Insulae provinciae sunt. — “Islands are provinces.” Both words here are plural forms, which is why the verb is sunt and not est. You'll study exactly how Latin builds plural endings starting next module — for now, treat this sentence as a complete memorized unit.
