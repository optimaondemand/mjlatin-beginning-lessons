Lesson 5.04
The Verb Must Match
Lesson
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
If the subject is plural, what happens to the verb?
In Latin the verb has to agree with its subject in number: one subject → est, more than one → sunt. Get this right and your sentences sound like a Roman's. Get it wrong and it's a real error — not “almost right.”
🎵 MEMORY WORK
The agreement rule: singular subject → est (is)  ·  plural subject → sunt (are).
Three tiny words to memorize: et (and) · sed (but) · non (not).
📚 PART 1 · ONE IS, MANY ARE
Puella est.
The girl is (here). — one subject
Puellae sunt.
The girls are (here). — plural subject
💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · NO WRONG ANSWER
In English, would you ever say “The girls is here”? What does your ear tell you?
📚 PART 2 · ET, SED, NON

These three little words never change their endings, and they show up on nearly every page of Latin.

et — and.   Porta et via sunt. = The gate and the road are (here).
sed — but.   Puella est, sed copiae non sunt. = The girl is here, but the troops are not.
non — not.   Latin puts non right before the verb: Copiae non sunt. = The troops are not (here).
💡 Watch out: when et joins two singular subjects, together they are plural — so the verb becomes sunt!   Porta et via sunt.
📚 PART 3 · THE READER'S CHECKLIST

When you read a Latin sentence, run this quick check:

1
Find the subject (the nominative noun). Is it singular or plural?
2
Check the verb. Does est/sunt match the subject's number?
3
Watch for et joining subjects (→ plural) and non before the verb.
✅ CHECK YOUR UNDERSTANDING
Choose the verb that agrees with the subject.
Puellae ___ .
Insula provincia ___ . (The island is a province.)
Porta ___ via ___ . (The gate and the road are here.)
Corona ___ . (corona = crown)

💪 ONE IDEA TO CARRY OUT

The verb always matches the subject's number: one → est, many → sunt. And et joining two subjects makes them plural. Say it: one is, many are.
