📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
How does Latin show that there is more than one?
In English you add -s to make a plural: girl → girls. Latin also changes the ending — but not to -s. Today you learn the ending Latin uses to say “more than one” when a noun is the subject.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to explain the difference between singular (one) and plural (more than one).
2
I will be able to make a 1st-declension subject plural by changing -a to -ae.
3
I will be able to spot a plural subject when I read a Latin sentence.

📚 PART 2 · ONE BECOMES MANY

The subject ending goes from -a to -ae

You already know that the nominative (subject) ending for a 1st-declension noun is -a. To make that subject plural — to talk about more than one — Latin changes the ending to -ae.

puella
one girl — the subject  👶
puellae
the girls — the subject  👶👶👶

The same swap works for every 1st-declension noun:

porta → portae (gates)
via → viae (roads)
insula → insulae (islands)
💡 Heads-up for later: you'll meet -ae doing a second job in Semester 2. For now the rule is simple: a 1st-declension subject ending in -ae means more than one.
