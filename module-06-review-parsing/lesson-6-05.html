📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Are you ready for the Module 6 Quiz?
No new grammar today — just pulling the week together: the parsing formula, the four functions, and the five-step checklist. Read a short passage the Roman way, play the Ludus, and take a self-check.
📚 PART 1 · THE WEEK ON ONE PAGE
The parsing formula: declension, case, number — always in that order.
SingularPlural
Nominative — subject / predicate nom.-a-ae
Accusative — direct object / obj. of prep.-am-ās
The checklist: verb → nominative → accusative → particles → write the parse.
Particles & prepositions: et, sed, non · ad, per, trāns, ante, post (+ accusative).
📚 PART 2 · READ IT THE ROMAN WAY

Run the five-step checklist on this short passage. Find each verb, each nominative, each accusative, and every particle before you translate:

Italia et Rōma provinciae non sunt; Rōma urbs magna est. (urbs, given: city) Nautae patriam amant, sed copiae ad Britanniam sunt.
Read it out: “Italy and Rome are not provinces; Rome is a great city. The sailors love the homeland, but the troops are near Britain.”
Italia and Rōma are nominative singular, joined by et as compound subjects of sunt (which is why the verb is plural, not est). Nautae is nominative plural, subject of amant. Patriam is accusative singular, direct object of amant. Britanniam is accusative singular, object of the preposition ad.
