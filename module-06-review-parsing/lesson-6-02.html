📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A case tells you the ending. What does it tell you about the job?
Naming the case and number is only half of parsing. The other half is naming the noun's job in the sentence — and here's the twist: each case can point to two different jobs. Today you chant the full paradigm and learn to tell those jobs apart.
🎵 MEMORY WORK
Chant the full paradigm, in order:
puella, puellam — puellae, puellās
Function labels to memorize: Nominative → subject OR predicate nominative. Accusative → direct object OR object of an accusative preposition.
📚 PART 1 · ONE CASE, TWO JOBS

A nominative noun is always the case's ending — but it can be doing either of two jobs. Compare:

Puella amat.
The girl loves. — subject: puella performs the action
Puella est nauta.
The girl is a sailor. — predicate nominative: nauta renames puella

And the accusative works the same way — two jobs, one ending:

Femina puellam amat.
The woman loves the girl. — direct object: puellam receives the action
Nauta ad terram est.
The sailor is near the land. — object of a preposition: terram follows ad
💡 How to tell the jobs apart: the ending only narrows it to two options. You decide which of the two by reading the rest of the sentence — is there a form of esse linking two nominatives? Is there a preposition right before the accusative noun?
📚 PART 2 · NAME THE JOB

Parse the bold noun, then decide which of its two possible jobs it's doing here.
