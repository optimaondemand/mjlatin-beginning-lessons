📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Are you ready for the Module 10 Quiz?
No new grammar today — just pulling the week together: principal parts, the stem, the three singular endings, and full sentences with a subject and an object. Read a short passage the Roman way, play the Ludus, and take a self-check.
📚 PART 1 · THE WEEK ON ONE PAGE
The paradigm — one pattern, every verb in this module:
PersonEndingamō
1st — I-ōamō
2nd — you-āsamās
3rd — he/she/it-atamat
The seven verbs: amō, laudō, portō, habitō, navigō, superō, laborō.
Sentence structure: subject (nominative) + object (accusative) + verb (agrees).
📚 PART 2 · READ IT THE ROMAN WAY

Run the reader's checklist on this short passage. Find each subject, check it's nominative, find each direct object, check it's accusative, and confirm the verb agrees:

Agricola terram amat et laborat. Agricola aquam portat. Nauta per aquam navigat, sed terram superat. Puella coronam laudat. Insulam puella non habitat; silvam habitat.
Read it out: “The farmer loves the land and works. The farmer carries water. The sailor sails through the water, but overcomes the land. The girl praises the crown. The girl does not dwell on the island; she dwells in the forest.”
Every subject here is singular and nominative, so every verb ends in -t. Watch terram and aquam and coronam and silvam and insulam — all accusative, all direct objects.
