📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Can you hold nouns, verbs, and prepositions in mind all at once?
Today's sentences are the toughest combination this semester offers: all three noun types, 1st conjugation verbs, and accusative-only prepositions (ad, in, per, trāns), all in the same sentence. You'll use a full parsing checklist — the same discipline Roman students used before they ever attempted a translation.

📚 PART 1 · THE COMPLETE PARSING CHECKLIST

Parsing means stating, systematically, every grammatical fact a Latin word's form tells you — before you translate a single word.

For a noun, ask:
1) Declension — check the dictionary ending (-ae, -ī)
2) Case — nominative or accusative?
3) Number — singular or plural?
4) Function — subject, direct object, or predicate nominative?
For a verb, ask:
1) Person — 1st, 2nd, or 3rd?
2) Number — singular or plural?
3) Tense — present (the only one you know)
4) Does it agree with its subject in number?
💡 Watch for: a preposition (ad, in, per, trāns) always takes its object in the accusative — so an accusative noun right after one of these four words is not a direct object of the verb.
