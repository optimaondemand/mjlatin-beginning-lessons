📚 PART 1 · THE ROMAN WORLD, IN REVIEW

Rome at the height of the Republic was a city of hundreds of thousands of people, organized around the forum — the public square where courts, temples, and markets clustered. City dwellers lived in insulae, apartment blocks that could rise six or seven stories. Outside the city, Roman roads (viae) radiated across Italy; the famous Via Appia, begun in 312 B.C., ran 350 Roman miles south to Brundisium.

The nauta worked the Mediterranean, which Romans called Mare Nostrum — “our sea.” The agricola worked the land. Many large farms (latifundia) were worked by enslaved laborers (servi) under an overseer. Your S1 grammar — nominative, accusative, and esse — is exactly what a Roman writer needed to describe who was acting and who was acted upon in this world.
