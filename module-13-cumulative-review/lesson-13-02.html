Lesson 13.02
Every Paradigm at Once
Lesson
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Can you reproduce all five S1 paradigms from memory?
Today you put every paradigm you own side by side: three noun tables, esse, and 1st conjugation. Before you read the tables below, take a blank sheet of paper and try to write all five from memory. Then check yourself against what's here. Mark any cell you got wrong — that's your target for the rest of this week.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to reproduce all three noun paradigms, esse, and 1st conjugation present tense from memory.
2
I will be able to retrieve the right noun or verb form quickly when given its job in a sentence.
3
I will be able to check a subject and its verb for number agreement without hesitating.
📋 WHAT YOU NEED FOR THIS LESSON
▶
A blank sheet of paper for the memory-first check
▶
Your Latin notebook, to check your work after

💡 Notice the parallel: the plural endings -mus, -tis, -nt show up in both esse and 1st conjugation verbs. That's not an accident — it's the same underlying pattern doing the same job twice.
📚 PART 2 · TARGETED RETRIEVAL PRACTICE

No looking back at the tables — pull each form from memory first, then check.

✅ CHECK YOUR UNDERSTANDING
Choose the correct form for each request.
The 1st person plural (“we love”) of amāre is…
The form that means “they are” is…
The accusative plural of dominus (master) is…
Which form is 3rd person singular: portat or portant?
📚 PART 3 · DOES THE VERB AGREE?

A plural subject needs a plural verb, in esse and in 1st conjugation alike. Check each pair.

✅ CHECK YOUR UNDERSTANDING
Does the subject and verb agree in number?
Servī dominum amat.
Oppidum magnum est. (magnum = large, given)
Puellae bonae sunt. (bonae = good, given)
