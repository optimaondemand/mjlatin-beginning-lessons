📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
What do you know with confidence, and what do you still need to work on?
No new grammar this week — this is Module 13, a cumulative review. You've spent twelve modules building one connected system: three noun paradigms, the verb esse, 1st conjugation verbs, and four little prepositions. This week you pull it all together, find your soft spots honestly, and drill them before the module quiz. Today: an honest self-check, and a fast recap of every noun type you know.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1

2
I will be able to honestly identify which parts of Semester 1 grammar feel solid and which need more practice.
3
I will be able to recognize five S1 vocabulary words through the English words they left behind.

📚 PART 1 · AN HONEST FIRST LOOK

Roman students had a saying: repetitio mater studiorum — repetition is the mother of learning. Before you review anything, take an honest look at where you stand right now. There's no wrong answer here — this is just you, checking in with yourself.

📚 PART 2 · THREE NOUN TYPES, ONE MORE TIME

You know three declensions. All three distinguish singular from plural, and all three distinguish nominative (subject) from accusative (object) — except the 2nd declension neuter, where nominative and accusative are always identical.

1st decl. — puella, -ae f.

Nom.puellapuellae

💡 The one to watch: neuter -a (like oppida) looks exactly like a 1st-declension singular subject. Context tells you which it is — but always check gender first.
